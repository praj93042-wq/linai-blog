## Context

Generative AI made it possible for any operator to ship a slick landing page or a polished design system. The unintended effect: every output looks the same. Designer Meng To, who has spent close to half a million dollars on AI tokens building products, argues that taste and a systematized workflow — not access to better models — separate work that converts from work that disappears into the feed.

## Purple Gradients Are the New AI Slop

Meng To, the designer behind Design+Code and a former Apple designer, has spent close to half a million dollars on AI tokens building products with the current generation of design tools. His diagnosis is that the problem is not the models. It is the workflow. Most people are stuck on the first generation step — type a prompt, accept the result, move on — and shipping the same cookie-cutter output as everyone else.

The fix is a single file: design.md. Google released the format publicly this year, and operators are using it as the blueprint that carries a curated visual system across every surface — landing page, motion design, slide decks, social cards, marketing video frames. One taste-laden file, infinite consistent outputs.

## Iterate 90% of the Time. Remix 10%.

The 6-step workflow is what separates Meng's output from the generic AI baseline. Reference: collect a tight set of designs you actually admire (not a generic Pinterest board). Generate: prompt with the references, not just the goal. Inspect: pull apart what the model produced and identify the three or four moves that made it work. Systematize: codify those moves into design.md — colors, type ratios, motion principles, copywriting voice, the specific kind of imperfection that makes the output feel human.

Then comes the part most people skip. Iterate: 1,000 small refinements against the design.md, each one tuning a detail rather than starting over. Remix: take the systematized design and adapt it to new mediums — the landing page becomes a slide, the slide becomes a 30-second motion piece, the motion piece becomes a paid ad. Meng's rule: 90% of the work is iteration, 10% is remix. The split inverts what most people do, which is generate-and-publish.

The shift in mental model matters more than the tool stack. AI did not eliminate the designer's job. It removed the friction between idea and first draft, which means the work that used to take three days now takes 30 minutes — and the bar for what counts as 'good' rises proportionally. Meng's framing: 'AI is not making me lazier. AI is making me work more.' The operators who treat AI as a labor-saving device produce slop. The operators who treat it as a magnification of their taste produce something distinguishable.

The common failure modes are concrete. Design drift — the output looking less like what you started with after each prompt — is the symptom of skipping the systematize step. Focusing on chat instead of decisions wastes the iteration budget. Letting AI 'do everything' without human inspection produces purple-gradient slop. The pattern that works is the opposite: AI handles volume, humans handle taste. Volume without taste is noise. Taste without volume is rare. The combination is the moat.
